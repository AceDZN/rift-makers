## Promise

A god game in deep time, in the style of From Dust and Reus, where you never touch the surface. On a floating slab of living planet, with a glowing **cutaway** down the side showing crust, mantle and core, you're the spirit of the mantle, and **heat is your only power**. Place a **hot plume** (rising mantle) or a **cold drip** (sinking mantle) under the slab, then let deep time run. The convection currents you created carry the plates away from plumes and toward drips. **Boundaries emerge where the flows meet. You never choose a boundary type.** Plates pulled apart over a plume crack, sink into a rift and flood into a new sea with stripes of young basalt. Over a drip, two continents crash and fold into mountains, or an ocean plate dives under a continent and leaves a trench and a ring of smoking volcanoes. Flows sliding past each other tear a river sideways. Your tiny villagers (the **חצצים**, pebble people) want things: a sea to fish, fertile volcanic soil, a hot spring, marble for a statue. They live where they live, and when they ask where to build a new town, you advise them from the plate map you made. Pick a site on a fault, or below a tsunami, and the quake or the wave knocks their houses down in a slapstick way they can recover from. The finale has you build the Land of Israel: the sandy kurkar coastal plain, the limestone mountain ridge whose marine fossils rose 700 m above the sea, the sinking rift with the lowest point on land, and Golan basalt.

**Not like the existing games.** Workbook's own module sims already cover these topics with direct manipulation. **PlateBoundaryLab** picks מתרחקים/מתנגשים/מחליקים and the crust types, then morphs the landform. **EarthLayersDrillLab** drills down with a depth slider and gauges. **RockCycleExplorer** clicks processes on a cycle wheel. **EarthquakeReadinessLab** sets a preparedness budget for Japan, Haiti and Israel, then runs a quake. **IsraelLandformBeltsLab** drags process chips onto belt slots. **Basin Breakers** (`~/dev/AceDZN-exp/basin-breakers`) lets you cut and raise terrain directly. Rift Makers must never use those verbs. No boundary-type picker, no depth slider, no rock-cycle buttons, no budget sliders, no process chips, no direct terrain sculpting, no free time scrubbing back and forth. Your verbs are: place heat under the crust, run deep time forward for a duration you commit to, read evidence (seismograph, rock samples, sea-floor ages), and advise villagers where to settle.

Map evidence: From Dust × Reus (related, density 0.681); From Dust × WorldBox (0.713) and Teardown × From Dust (0.761, crowded) are the alternatives. Keep it different from River Carver (a reserve idea): here you shape the planet from inside. Water and wind only show up as external processes.

Rules, from the modules (pure TS; the heightfield is generated from this state):
- **Structure:** crust (קרום), mantle (מעטפת) with magma (מגמה), liquid outer core and solid inner core (solid because of pressure, though hotter). Scientists "see" inside with seismic waves (גלי הדף, a seismograph), and that's how we know the outer core is liquid. The magnetic field is tied to the core. **Internal vs. external processes:** internal ones (tectonics, volcanism) build relief; external ones (weathering, erosion, deposition) wear it down.
- **Plate tectonics:** convection currents in the mantle (זרמי ערבול) drive the plates at **2–7 cm per year**: slow, but millions of years move continents thousands of km (game math: distance = rate × time, shown on the slider). There are three boundary types, each with its own landforms:
  - **Divergent (מתרחקים):** a mid-ocean ridge (the Mid-Atlantic), a new sea floor that's younger near the ridge, and rift valleys.
  - **Convergent (מתקרבים):** continent–continent makes fold mountains (the Himalaya is still rising, while the old Appalachians are low and eroded); ocean under continent is **subduction** (סובדוקציה): trench, magma, a volcanic arc and strong quakes (the Ring of Fire around the Pacific).
  - **Transform (מחליקים זה לצד זה):** earthquakes, offset features.
  - Wegener's continental drift: the evidence (fitting coastlines, fossils, rocks) and why the scientific critique mattered.
- **The Syrian-African rift (הבקע הסורי-אפריקני):** the African and Arabian plates. **Workbook uses two framings:** plate-tectonics says the plates "move apart (~5–7 mm/yr)", and landform-belts says they "move side by side and the ground between them sank". Teach exactly the Workbook wording in each level and don't contradict either. The game can model the Dead Sea rift as plates sliding past each other with a sinking block between them, which shows both facts. It explains the Jordan Valley, the Kinneret, the Dead Sea (the lowest point on land), the Gulf of Eilat, Israel's earthquakes and the 1927 Jordan Valley quake.
- **Earthquakes and volcanoes:** stress builds along boundaries and is released as a quake; magma erupts there too, so both cluster along plate boundaries. Focus vs. epicenter (מוקד, מוקד-על if the module uses it; check). **Richter** measures energy objectively: 2 units = ~100× the shaking and ~1,000× the energy (module). **Mercalli** measures damage, which depends on construction. That's why Haiti's 7.0 killed far more than Japan's 9.0. Quakes can't be predicted precisely; Israel prepares with **Standard 413** (תקן 413, earthquake-resistant construction required for new buildings; it reduces damage and doesn't predict quakes). Tsunami (צונאמי).
- **Rocks and the rock cycle:** igneous (יסוד: basalt cools fast at the surface, granite slowly underground), sedimentary (משקע: limestone, chalk, fossils form under seas), metamorphic (תמורה: marble from limestone under heat and pressure); one cycle connecting them all. Marine fossils in the Jerusalem hills, 700+ m up, mean an ancient sea and later uplift.
- **Israel's landform belts:** the coastal plain (מישור החוף), sand and kurkar ridges carried by rivers, sea and wind (deposition); the mountain ridge (אזור ההר), limestone and chalk, the national watershed (קו פרשת המים) with streams flowing west to the Mediterranean and east to the Jordan rift; the rift (הבקע), a sunken block hundreds of metres below sea level. Golan basalt points to volcanism.
- Hebrew terms: קרום, מעטפת, גלעין חיצוני / פנימי, מגמה, לבה, לוח טקטוני, גבול לוחות, זרמי ערבול, רכס מרכז-אוקייני, סובדוקציה, שבר, בקע, נדידת היבשות, רעידת אדמה, מוקד, סולם ריכטר, סולם מרקלי, תקן 413, צונאמי, הר געש, טבעת האש, סלעי יסוד / משקע / תמורה, מחזור הסלעים, מאובן, בזלת, גיר, קירטון, שיש, כורכר, רצועות האורך, פרשת מים, תהליכים פנימיים / חיצוניים.

Misconceptions to catch: plates move fast or only during quakes; the crust "floats on lava"; all mountains are volcanoes; every collision makes volcanoes (continent–continent mostly doesn't); a higher magnitude always means more deaths; quakes can be predicted precisely; fossils on mountains mean the sea once rose that high (rather than the land rising); every rock is the same "stone"; the core is liquid all the way through.

## Loop and controls

- **Heat:** an isometric/orbit camera over the slab, with the mantle visible through the cutaway and through a translucent "heat view" toggle. Place a limited number of **hot plumes** and **cold drips** on the mantle grid under the slab (the level sets how many). Convection ribbons show the flow you made. The plates are rigid pieces of oceanic or continental crust given by the level; you can't change them.
- **Deep time:** commit a duration (for example "run 20 million years"), then watch it play out fast with the HUD showing rate × time = km moved. Time only runs forward. Restarting the level keeps your heat placements so you can adjust and rerun. The terrain, sea, volcanoes, faults and rock layers come from the pure model.
- **Read the planet:** a seismograph network (which waves arrive where), a rock hammer (take a sample from any spot on the cutaway: igneous/sedimentary/metamorphic, with or without fossils) and sea-floor age stripes.
- **Advise the villagers:** the חצצים ask where to put a new town, a harbor, a hot-spring bath or a quarry. You choose the site once on the map, and then time or the quake decides. Houses built after the Standard 413 quest are sturdier. That's a one-time story unlock, never a budget to allocate.
- **Consequences:** wrong heat placement means the wrong landform. Villagers who wanted a sea get a mountain and sulk with fishing rods on a peak. A town placed on a boundary gets slapstick collapses and dust, then villagers climbing out and rebuilding (never death or gore). Each comes with a replay of the cutaway flow and one Hebrew line ("the drip pulled two continents together; no plate sank, so there are no volcanoes").
- Hint (logged, costs a star): highlights the evidence (heat flow, fossil, rock type, ages of the sea floor) and never where to place the heat.

## Levels (author exactly; tests prove each; 18 total)

**World 1 — Inside the Earth · בבטן כדור הארץ** (earth-structure, plate-tectonics)
1. **The first plume:** a sleepy slab and a villager with a fishing rod: "אנחנו רוצים ים!" Place one hot plume under the middle, run 10 million years, and the land splits, sinks and floods (tutorial: flow arrows shown).
2. **Liquid or solid:** seismograph puzzle. Place three stations around the planet, trigger a quake, see which waves never cross the outer core, and mark the liquid layer. The marked layer has to match the data; the cutaway then reveals it.
3. **Which way?** One plume and one drip are already burning. Predict (commit) which way each of the two plates will move before running time; the villagers' boats are on the line.
4. **Slow but sure:** the villagers want their two shores 300 km apart for a fishing season. The plates move 5 cm per year: commit the number of years (6 million) and run it once. Too short leaves a ditch; too long and the harbor drifts out of reach.
5. **Transfer, the quiet planet:** a new planet with only heat-flow readings and seismograph data. Find where the mantle engine already rises and place one plume to open the first rift exactly where the villagers need a sea.

**World 2 — Plates on the Move · לוחות בתנועה** (plate-tectonics)
6. **Zebra sea floor:** a wide ocean with basalt age stripes. The villagers' oldest treasure is buried in the oldest crust: read the stripes (youngest at the ridge) and send the dig to the right edge.
7. **Continent meets continent:** two continents and one drip. Fold the Himalaya: no volcanoes, the peak keeps rising. An old range beside it has eroded low, so the villagers' snow festival needs the young one.
8. **Ocean dives under:** an ocean plate and a continent. Place the drip so the trench is offshore and the volcanic arc (fertile soil) forms under the farmers' fields, not under the town.
9. **Slide past:** two flows slipping past each other tear a river and a road sideways. Run just enough time to bring the two halves of a split village back together.
10. **Transfer, Wegener's puzzle:** the continents are scattered. Move the pieces back together using coastline fit, matching fossils and rock belts, then let your heat run time forward to today. The critics (rigged scientist NPCs) only accept the fit when all the evidence matches.

**World 3 — Shake and Burn · רועד ובוער** (earthquakes-volcanoes)
11. **Ring of Fire:** an ocean ringed by villages. Close the ocean with drips so the trenches and volcanoes all form offshore, then watch the ring of fire light up around it.
12. **Where to build?** The plate map from your last level, and a growing village asking for a new town site. Quakes and volcanoes cluster on boundaries: choose the site, run 1,000 years of quakes and see.
13. **Bigger isn't deadlier:** two coastal towns hit by two quakes 2 units apart on Richter (about 100× the shaking, ~1,000× the energy). The town with 413 houses takes the bigger one better. Choose which town's villagers should copy which, then watch the Mercalli damage map. No budget sliders.
14. **Tsunami:** a subduction trench offshore. Choose the harbor site and the high-ground path before the quake. The wave only spares the right choice.
15. **Transfer, Jordan Valley 1927:** the historical rift quake. The villagers rebuild after it: choose sites for the valley towns and Jerusalem's new quarter and unlock Standard 413. Quakes can't be predicted precisely, so the stars come from placement and construction, not timing.

**World 4 — Rocks and the Land of Israel · סלעים וארץ ישראל** (rocks-minerals, landform-belts)
16. **Rock orders:** the villagers order basalt for millstones, granite for a tower, limestone for houses and marble for a statue. Make the planet produce them: eruptions at a rift (basalt), magma cooling deep under a mountain belt and exposed by time (granite), a long shallow sea (limestone with fossils), and limestone squeezed in a collision (marble). Then collect each one with the rock hammer.
17. **Fossils at 700 m:** the villagers find seashells on a mountain and think the sea was 700 m deep. Run a shallow sea, then the uplift, and show the strata in the cutaway: the land rose, the sea didn't.
18. **Final, building the Land of Israel (no hints):** a slab between the sea and the desert, with the African and Arabian plates. With heat and deep time, create the rift (plates sliding past each other with the block between them sinking to the lowest point on land), the limestone ridge (the watershed, with streams going west and east), the kurkar coastal plain (deposition from the sea, rivers and wind, which the model runs on its own as external processes) and Golan basalt. Then choose safe 413 town sites before the final rift quake. Nothing is labeled.

After the authored levels work, add generated "planet commissions" from a solver (villager wishes + a constraint). Verify each is solvable, and verify that "always one plume in the middle" and random heat placement fail.
